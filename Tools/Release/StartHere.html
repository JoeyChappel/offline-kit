Offline Kit

A family reference library that works with no internet: ask questions, read Wikipedia and repair guides, listen to audiobooks and play games.

You need: a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer) running macOS 26 or later, and room for what you download: about 6 GB for the app and its AI model, plus whatever you choose for the library.

1. Install

  1. Open the disk image and drag Offline Kit onto the Applications folder.
  2. Open Offline Kit from Applications. The first time, macOS asks to confirm opening an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open.
  3. If macOS instead says it can't verify the app, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.

2. Get your library (pick one)

  • From a drive: if you were given an Offline Kit drive, plug it in and open Offline Kit from the drive's OfflineKit folder. Everything is already on it. When macOS asks whether Offline Kit may use files on a removable volume, click Allow.
  • Download it yourself (needs internet once): open Get Resources. Pick where to save, choose areas such as Emergency, Health and Home, tick what you want and click Download. Downloads resume if they're interrupted and are checked for errors when they finish.

3. Turn on answers

Ask uses an AI model that runs on your Mac, not on the internet. In Get Resources, get Gemma 4 E4B (5.15 GB) from the Built-in AI model card. If your Mac has Apple Intelligence turned on, Ask also works without it, with shorter answers.

4. What's inside

  • Ask: type a question; the answer cites the articles it came from. Click a number to read the source.
  • Library: browse by area, topic and guide, or search every archive.
  • Watch & Listen: your own videos and audio, plus free classic audiobooks for kids. It remembers where you stopped.
  • Prepare: a family plan, wallet cards, supplies with expiry reminders and a power-out mode that times your fridge and freezer.
  • Games: Game Boy, Game Boy Color, Game Boy Advance, NES, PC Engine, DOS and ScummVM games you add.
  • Share on Wi-Fi: lets phones and tablets in the house use the library from a web browser.
Please double-check anything medical, electrical or safety-related against the cited article, and get professional help when you can. Answers come from your offline library where it covers the question, and anything else is labeled “Not from the library.” They can still be wrong.

Content comes from Kiwix (Wikipedia, WikiMed, iFixit, Stack Exchange and more) and LibriVox. Licenses vary by archive; Wikipedia and Stack Exchange use CC BY-SA.
